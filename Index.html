How a flip works

The money is made when you buy, not when you sell. Four steps, repeated.

  1. Find

    Search misspelt brands, "job lot", "bundle", and listings with bad photos. Sellers who don't know what they have price it low.

  2. Buy

    Only buy if the sold price (not the asking price) leaves at least £8 profit after postage. Offer 10–20% under.

  3. List

    Wash it. Shoot it in daylight on a plain wall. Add measurements, the brand and honest flaws. Good photos sell the same item for more.

  4. Ship

    Post within 48 hours, packaged neatly. Fast shipping gets you good reviews, and reviews get you sales.

Signs a listing is flippable

  • Misspelt brand. "Carhart" or "Levis jaket" doesn't show up in normal searches.
  • Bad photos. Dark, creased, on the floor. A clean re-shoot adds value.
  • Bundles and clear-outs. Sellers price a whole bag low. Resell each piece.
  • Wrong category. A vintage tee listed under "other" gets few views.
  • Listed in the last hour. Cheap listings go fast, so sort by newest.
  • Check it's real. Look at the stitching, tags and labels. Skip anything you can't verify.

Starting on £50–£100

Small budget, so the plan is: low risk, fast turnover, reinvest everything.

  • Buy items under £10Cheap enough that a mistake doesn't hurt. Aim to sell for 2–3 times what you paid.
  • Stick to what sellsVintage sportswear, fleeces, Carhartt, Levi's, Nike, Adidas, Stone Island, band tees. Check sold listings first.
  • Check sold, not askingOn eBay, filter by "Sold items". On Vinted and Depop, look for how fast similar items vanish.
  • Reinvest your first profitsDon't spend the first £100 you make. Roll it back into stock until you have a steady supply.
